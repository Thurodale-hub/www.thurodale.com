The problem

Order in the records, control in the structure.

Administration slips quietly. A resolution is signed and never filed. A transfer completes and the register still shows last year’s holders. An annual return passes while everyone assumes someone else has it in hand.

None of it fails loudly. It accumulates, until a lender, an auditor or a buyer asks to see the minute book.

We keep the record straight: registers current, obligations visible, and every corporate change carried through to the page it belongs on.

Questions

Common questions.

Who do you work with?

Private companies, holding structures, family offices and multi-entity groups. Anyone whose records need to be right and current, rather than reconstructed under pressure.

Our records are already behind. Can you help?

Yes. Minute book review and remediation is standing work. We rebuild incomplete records from your instructions and what is available, working alongside your lawyers and other advisors wherever their input is required, then leave you a structure that can be maintained.

Can you support us through a restructuring or transaction?

Yes. We handle the records and documentation side of structural change: reorganizations, share transfers, amalgamations, continuances and post-closing integration. We prepare and circulate the documents, track what is outstanding, and update the registers so the record reflects the new structure once the work is done.

Can you set up a new entity?

Yes, on a project basis. We handle formation, the initial records setup and the governance framework, then maintain the entity from that point on. Structuring decisions normally sit with your legal counsel; we carry out the administration and keep the record clean from the first day rather than reconstructing it later.

How do you keep several entities compliant at once?

We track what each entity owes and when: annual filings, resolutions, register updates and renewals. Where an entity sits outside Ontario, we coordinate with the local registry or agent. You get one point of contact and one view of what is current, what is due and what has been filed, rather than chasing each entity separately.

How does an engagement start?

Under a written scope and a formal agreement. Work begins once onboarding is complete and the services, assumptions and fees are confirmed.
